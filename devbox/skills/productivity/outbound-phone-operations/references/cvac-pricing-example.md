# Worked Example: CVAC Pricing Inquiry

> Dated evidence from 2026-08-18. This file demonstrates call recovery and reporting discipline; it is not a permanently current price sheet.

## Goal

Call CVAC Systems and determine the purchase price of one CVAC pod/system.

Official contact page supplied two useful routes:

- toll-free: `866-753-2822`
- corporate: `951-699-2086`

The requested outcome was a factual answer, so a voicemail alone did not satisfy the task.

## Route behavior

The main IVR offered technical support, sessions, accounting, consultants, technology questions, purchasing, and “all other calls.”

1. The toll-free line’s consultant route reached an automated mailbox.
2. The mailbox required a post-recording keypad confirmation. This demonstrates why a voicemail branch must listen after speaking and explicitly save the message.
3. A second call used the corporate number and selected the general/operator route rather than repeating the consultant mailbox.
4. That route reached Carl, a live CVAC representative.
5. The AI assistant disclosed its identity and transcription. Carl explicitly consented before the pricing discussion.

General lesson: after a department mailbox dead-end, use a distinct official route or general operator during business hours. Do not redial the same mailbox repeatedly.

## Verified facts from the live representative

Carl stated:

- retail price for one system: **$148,000**;
- exclusions: shipping/freight, installation, and tax;
- maintenance: the system is designed to require little maintenance, with the HEPA filter changed annually;
- warranty: five years, covering everything except upholstery;
- lead time: three to six months;
- manufacturing note: the system is hand-built.

Carl did not quote a dollar amount for recurring maintenance or service. The precise report is therefore “no recurring service-fee amount was quoted,” not “there are no recurring fees.”

## Transcript-quality lessons

Automatic speech recognition rendered CVAC inconsistently as “SeaVac,” “CVACC,” “CVX,” and similar variants. Because the call went to CVAC’s official number and the live representative identified the business, normalizing the company/product name to **CVAC** in the report was justified. The price and exclusions were repeated clearly enough to report.

The first voicemail transcript also garbled an email address. For short vendor voicemails, prefer one callback number. If email is unavoidable, instruct the assistant to spell the local part and domain slowly and then verify it in the transcript.

## Completion judgment

The provider marked the voicemail attempt successful, but it did not answer the user’s question. Completion came only after the second call produced a live, consented transcript containing the price and terms.

When reusing this example:

- re-verify all prices and commercial terms;
- preserve the route-recovery pattern, not the dated commercial answer;
- report only facts explicitly stated in the new transcript.
